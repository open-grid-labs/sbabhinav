Pre-Wedding Shoot in Spiti, Lahaul & Sissu: Planning Guide

21 November 2026

Home / Blog

If you want your pre-wedding photos to look like nowhere else in India, look beyond the green hills to the cold desert of Lahaul and Spiti. Bare brown mountains, turquoise rivers, whitewashed monasteries and enormous skies make for dramatic, cinematic frames. Since the Atal Tunnel opened, Sissu and Lahaul are a short drive from Manali. Here's how to plan a pre-wedding shoot in Sissu, Lahaul and Spiti, and what to watch out for at high altitude.

Sissu and the Atal Tunnel side (Lahaul)

The Atal Tunnel takes you from the green Kullu valley to the stark Lahaul valley in minutes. On the far side, Sissu is an easy day trip from Manali.

  • Sissu waterfall and the Chandra river: a long waterfall coming down the cliffs, with the river and open fields in front
  • Sissu lake and helipad area: wide, open land with snow peaks all around
  • Villages around Keylong: traditional Lahauli houses, fields and monasteries such as Kardang and Shashur

Sissu is one of the best places in Himachal for a landscape-heavy pre-wedding shoot without a multi-day trip. Mornings are clearest.

Spiti Valley

Spiti is much more remote: Kaza, its main town, is a long day's drive from Manali through Kunzum Pass when the road is open, or a two-day drive via Shimla and Kinnaur. It's a commitment, but the locations are extraordinary:

  • Key Monastery: a thousand-year-old monastery stacked on a hill above the valley, one of the most iconic sights in the Himalayas
  • Chicham bridge: one of the highest bridges in Asia, over a deep gorge
  • Langza: a tiny village with a huge Buddha statue looking out over the peaks
  • Kibber and Hikkim: high villages with sweeping views; Hikkim has one of the world's highest post offices
  • Chandratal Lake: a crescent-shaped lake at about 4,300 metres, its colour changing with the light. Reachable roughly June to early October.
  • Dhankar: a dramatic old monastery on a cliff edge above the confluence of the Spiti and Pin rivers

When to go

  • June to early October: the only reliable window. The Manali–Kaza road via Kunzum and the Chandratal road are usually open.
  • Late September to early October: our favourite. Clear skies, golden fields and fewer travellers.
  • November to May: very cold. Kunzum is closed and Spiti is reachable only via Kinnaur. Sissu can still be reached through the Atal Tunnel for snow shoots, depending on conditions.

Permits and rules

  • Atal Tunnel and Sissu: no special permit is needed for Indian tourists to drive through the tunnel to Sissu at the time of writing, but rules change with season and traffic, so check before you travel.
  • Rohtang Pass: if your route goes over Rohtang instead of through the tunnel, vehicles need an online permit, and the number of permits per day is limited. The pass closes in winter.
  • Spiti: Indian citizens don't need a permit for the usual Spiti circuit. Foreign nationals need an inner line permit for some stretches near the border in Kinnaur and Spiti.
  • Monasteries: always ask before photographing inside. Many don't allow photography inside prayer halls at all.
  • Drones: high-altitude and border-adjacent areas can be sensitive. Read our permits and drone rules guide before planning aerial shots.

Altitude and safety

Spiti and Lahaul are above 3,000 metres, and Chandratal is higher still. Altitude sickness is real, and it affects how you feel and how you look in photos.

  • Acclimatise: spend a night at a middle altitude before going high, and don't rush to Chandratal or Kibber on day one
  • Hydrate, avoid alcohol for the first day or two, and talk to your doctor if you have health concerns
  • Keep shoots short at the highest points, and keep warm layers handy even in summer
  • Plan for limited network and few fuel stations. Travel with an experienced local driver.

What to wear in the cold desert

The landscape is brown, grey and white, with blue skies and turquoise water, so colour pops: red, maroon, mustard, emerald, royal blue and orange all look stunning. Flowing fabrics catch the wind beautifully. Wear sturdy shoes to walk in and change at the spot. See our mountain outfit guide for more.

Sample plans

Sissu day trip from Manali

  1. Leave Manali early, cross the Atal Tunnel
  2. Morning: Sissu waterfall and river, while light is soft and skies are clear
  3. Midday: Lahauli village lanes and fields
  4. Afternoon: return to Manali for a golden hour session in the orchards

Spiti, four to five days

  1. Day 1: Manali to Kaza via Atal Tunnel and Kunzum (long drive, rest on arrival)
  2. Day 2: acclimatise in Kaza; short evening session nearby
  3. Day 3: Key Monastery at sunrise, Kibber and Chicham bridge
  4. Day 4: Langza and Hikkim, golden hour at Langza
  5. Day 5: Chandratal on the way back, if open

Cost

A Spiti shoot costs more than a hill-station shoot because of the extra days, a dedicated vehicle and driver, and stays for the team. A Sissu day trip, on the other hand, can be added to a Manali shoot for little more than one extra day's travel. See our Manali pre-wedding cost guide and our pre-wedding shoot prices in Himachal.

Frequently Asked Questions

When can you do a pre-wedding shoot in Spiti?

June to early October, when the roads via Kunzum Pass and to Chandratal are usually open. Late September to early October is the best balance of clear skies and fewer travellers.

Do Indians need a permit for Spiti or Sissu?

Indian citizens generally don't need a permit for Sissu or the usual Spiti circuit; foreign nationals need inner line permits for some border stretches. Rohtang Pass needs a vehicle permit. Rules change, so check before travelling.

Can we do a Sissu pre-wedding shoot as a day trip from Manali?

Yes. Through the Atal Tunnel, Sissu is an easy day trip from Manali, and it can be combined with a golden hour session in Manali's orchards on the way back.
