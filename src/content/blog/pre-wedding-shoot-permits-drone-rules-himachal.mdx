**Short answer:** you don't need a permit for a casual pre-wedding shoot in most public places in Himachal Pradesh, such as Mall Roads, viewpoints, forest roads and village lanes. You **do** need permission for private property (orchards, hotels, tea estates), many heritage and government sites, railway premises, protected forests and wildlife sanctuaries, and for flying a drone almost anywhere. Rules change often, so check the current position before your shoot. Here's what to know.

## Public places

Roads, viewpoints, Mall Roads, ghats and most forest trails are generally fine for a small shoot with a photographer and a couple. Things that can change that:

- **Large setups:** big crews, lights, stands, props or a set-up that blocks the path can attract objections from the local administration or police. Keep it small and quick.
- **Crowds:** if you're blocking tourists or traffic, you'll be asked to move. Shoot early.
- **Local customs:** some village and temple areas are sensitive about photography. Ask before you shoot.

## Private property

Many of the most beautiful spots in Himachal are privately owned:

- **Apple orchards** in Shimla, Kullu and Manali, and **tea estates** in Palampur and Kangra
- **Hotels, resorts and heritage properties** like palaces and colonial cottages
- **Golf courses**, such as Naldehra

Always ask the owner or management first. Some charge a shoot fee; many hotels allow shoots for guests staying with them. Never walk into an orchard or estate without asking.

## Temples, monasteries and churches

- Shoot exteriors respectfully, and avoid prayer and service times
- Ask before photographing inside, and accept a no gracefully. Many monasteries and temples don't allow photography inside.
- Dress modestly at religious sites and don't pose in ways that might offend worshippers
- Never photograph monks, nuns, priests or worshippers without their permission

## Heritage sites and government buildings

Sites managed by the Archaeological Survey of India or the state government, such as some old forts, temples and colonial buildings, may need prior permission or a fee for professional photography, and may restrict tripods. Some government buildings and areas don't allow photography at all.

## Railways

Shoots on the Kalka–Shimla heritage railway's stations, platforms or trains need permission from the railway authorities. Public viewpoints near the line are a simpler option.

## Protected forests and wildlife sanctuaries

Places like Kalatop near Dalhousie and the Great Himalayan National Park area have entry rules, fees and restrictions. Check with the forest department before planning a shoot inside.

## Restricted and sensitive areas

- **Cantonments and Air Force areas:** Kasauli's Monkey Point and parts of Dagshai and other cantonment towns restrict photography.
- **Border-adjacent areas:** parts of Kinnaur and Spiti are close to the border. Foreign nationals need inner line permits, and drones are especially sensitive here.

## Rohtang Pass

If you want to shoot at Rohtang Pass, vehicles need an online permit, the number of permits per day is limited, and the pass is closed in winter. Many couples now go through the Atal Tunnel to Sissu instead. See our [Spiti and Lahaul shoot guide](/blog/pre-wedding-shoot-spiti-lahaul-sissu/).

## Drone rules for weddings and pre-wedding shoots

Drone shots of a mountain venue or a couple in a valley are spectacular, but drones in India are regulated by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) under the Drone Rules, 2021. In practice:

- **The drone and the pilot must comply with DGCA rules,** including registration on the Digital Sky platform and a remote pilot certificate for commercial work
- **Check the airspace map:** Digital Sky shows green, yellow and red zones. Yellow and red zones, such as near airports (Shimla, Kullu–Bhuntar and Kangra–Gaggal) and sensitive installations, need permission or are off-limits.
- **Venue permission:** most hotels and resorts have their own drone policy. Ask in writing.
- **Local administration and police:** in tourist hotspots and around crowds, local authorities may ask you to stop flying even in a green zone
- **Weather and wind:** mountain winds are strong and unpredictable. A responsible pilot won't fly if it isn't safe.
- **Never fly over crowds** of guests at a wedding, or over monasteries and temples without permission

Drone rules are updated from time to time, so check the current DGCA guidance before your date. ## A simple permissions checklist

1. List every location for your shoot
2. Mark each one: public, private, religious, heritage, government, protected or restricted
3. Contact owners and managers for private and heritage locations
4. Check Digital Sky and the venue's policy if you want drone shots
5. Carry ID, and any permission letters, on the day
6. Keep a backup location for anything that isn't confirmed
