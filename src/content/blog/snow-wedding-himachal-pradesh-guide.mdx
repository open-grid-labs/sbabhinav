**Short answer:** for a snow wedding or snow pre-wedding shoot in Himachal Pradesh, plan for **late December to mid-February**, and choose places that get reliable snow but stay reachable by road: **Kufri and Narkanda near Shimla, Solang Valley near Manali, and Dalhousie**. Snowfall varies a lot from year to year, so the single most important thing is a flexible backup date. Here's how to plan it properly.

## Where to find snow in Himachal

### Near Shimla

- **Kufri:** about 30–45 minutes from Shimla and the easiest snow location for most couples. It gets very busy by late morning, so shoot early.
- **Narkanda:** about two hours from Shimla, higher and quieter, with apple orchards and forests that hold snow longer than Kufri.
- **The Ridge and Mall Road:** after a fresh snowfall, central Shimla itself looks spectacular for an hour or two before it gets crowded and slushy.

### Near Manali

- **Solang Valley:** the most dependable snow near Manali through winter, with big mountain backdrops.
- **Gulaba and Kothi:** on the road towards Rohtang, often snowy in winter, though access depends on the day's road conditions.
- **Sethan:** stunning in snow, but the road can be tricky. We only plan it when conditions allow.

### Elsewhere

- **Dalhousie and Khajjiar:** pine forests and meadows under snow, a quieter alternative to Shimla and Manali.
- **Dharamshala area:** the town rarely gets heavy snow, but the Dhauladhar range behind it is white all winter, which makes a beautiful backdrop without the cold.

## Snow wedding vs snow pre-wedding shoot

A **snow pre-wedding shoot** is the easier plan. It's two people, a photographer and a couple of outfits, so you can move the date by a day or two to follow the snow.

A **snow wedding** is a bigger commitment, because guests, catering and decor can't follow the forecast. If you want a winter wedding in the hills:

- Choose a venue with a solid **indoor or covered option** for every function
- Pick a venue below the heaviest snow line, so guests can actually reach it, and plan a short couple portrait session higher up
- Make sure the venue has heating, power backup and snow-cleared access roads
- Tell guests to bring proper winter wear, and plan transport with chains or 4x4 vehicles on standby

## What can go wrong, and how we plan around it

- **No snow on the day:** snowfall isn't guaranteed even in January. We track forecasts from a week out and keep a second date free.
- **Too much snow:** heavy snowfall closes roads, particularly beyond Solang and around Narkanda. We pick locations we can reach and get back from safely.
- **Cold:** photos look best when you're not shivering. Thermal layers under outfits, hand warmers, a heated car between setups, and short, focused sessions.
- **Glare and exposure:** snow reflects a lot of light. Early morning and late afternoon give softer light and better skin tones than midday.
- **Wet outfits and shoes:** bring a pair of boots to walk in, and change into your shoot shoes at the spot.

## What to wear in the snow

- **Rich colours stand out against white:** deep red, emerald, royal blue, mustard, burgundy
- **Avoid pure white and very pale pastels** unless you want a soft, monochrome look
- **Layer smartly:** a long coat, a shawl or a fur stole can be part of the outfit, not something hidden
- **Lehengas and long gowns photograph beautifully in snow,** but carry a backup outfit in case the hem gets soaked

## Is a snow shoot more expensive?

Usually a little, because of the extra planning: a backup day, longer drives and sometimes 4x4 hire. Peak winter hotel rates in Shimla and Manali also climb around Christmas and New Year. Our [Manali pre-wedding cost guide](/blog/pre-wedding-shoot-cost-manali/) explains the cost in more detail.
