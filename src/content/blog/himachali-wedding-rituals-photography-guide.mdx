A Himachali wedding moves differently from a big city wedding. It's usually smaller and more family-centred, built around age-old rituals, the maternal uncle (mama), the local deities and a shared meal, the dham. For a photographer, that means knowing which moments matter before they happen. Here's a guide to the main Himachali (Pahari) wedding rituals and how we photograph each one, from a studio that's based in Sundernagar and shoots these weddings every season.

Customs vary a lot between districts, communities and families. Kangra, Mandi, Kullu, Shimla, Chamba and the Gaddi and Kinnauri communities each have their own variations, so always check the order of events with the family. This guide covers the moments you'll see at most Himachali Hindu weddings.

## Before the wedding

### Roka and sagai

The roka is the formal "yes" between the two families, often small and held at home. The sagai (engagement) follows with the exchange of rings and gifts. These are intimate events, so we keep the kit light, work with available light and focus on the families' reactions as much as the couple.

### Batna (haldi)

Haldi is one of the most joyful parts of a Himachali wedding. Relatives apply turmeric paste to the bride or groom, usually at home, often in the courtyard. It gets messy and loud, and that's the point.

- **What we photograph:** the first application by the mother, grandmothers and mama; the chaos and laughter; close-ups of turmeric on hands and faces; the ritual bath afterwards
- **Tip:** wear old clothes if you're a guest, and give the photographer room near the bride or groom

### Mehendi and sangeet

Henna for the bride and the women of the family, with folk songs and dancing. Many Himachali families sing traditional Pahari songs and dance the Nati, a slow circle dance where everyone joins hands. The Nati is one of the most photogenic moments of any Himachali wedding. We shoot it wide to show the whole circle and then tight on faces.

## The wedding day

### Mama's role and gifts

In many Himachali weddings the bride's maternal uncle has a central role, from bringing gifts and clothes for the bride to leading her to the mandap. Families often describe it as one of the most emotional moments of the day. We make sure we know who the mama is before the day starts.

### The nath (nose ring)

The large, ornate gold nath is a signature of the Himachali bride and often a family heirloom. It deserves its own detail shots and portraits, ideally in soft window light while the bride is getting ready, before the rush of the ceremony.

### Baraat and the groom's arrival

The groom arrives with his family, often with a band or local musicians playing traditional instruments. In some communities the groom arrives on horseback. We usually split the team here, with one photographer covering the baraat and another with the bride, so neither moment is missed.

### Milni and jaimala

The two families greet each other, uncles and fathers embrace, and the couple exchange garlands. It's fast and crowded, so we position ourselves before it starts.

### Lagan and pheras

The main ceremony is led by the pandit around the sacred fire. In the hills this often happens late at night or before dawn, depending on the auspicious muhurat, and sometimes in a family courtyard rather than a hall. We plan for low light: fast lenses, discreet lighting and as little flash as possible, so the ritual isn't disturbed.

### Kanyadaan and sindoor

Two of the most emotional moments. We stay still and close, and let the moment happen.

## After the ceremony

### Vidaai

The bride's farewell from her family home. In smaller towns and villages the whole neighbourhood often comes out. It's rarely posed and always emotional.

### The dham

The traditional Himachali feast, cooked by botis (traditional cooks) and served on leaf plates (pattal) while guests sit in rows on the floor. Dishes vary by region, from Kangri dham to Mandyali dham: madra, rajmah, kadi, mash dal, khatta and meetha bhaat. The dham is a huge part of a Himachali wedding, so we photograph the cooking, the serving and the guests, not just the couple.

### Welcoming the bride

At the groom's home the bride is welcomed with rituals at the doorway and games that break the ice between the families.

## How to plan your photography for a Himachali wedding

- **Share the full schedule early,** including the muhurat time, which may be late at night
- **Tell us who the key people are,** especially the mama, grandparents and anyone travelling from far away
- **Plan for two photographers** if the baraat and the bride's preparation overlap
- **Mention any family heirlooms,** such as the nath, old jewellery or a family dupatta, so we give them proper detail shots
- **Ask about drone coverage early,** as it depends on the venue and local rules (see our [permits and drone guide](/blog/pre-wedding-shoot-permits-drone-rules-himachal/))
