**Short answer:** a destination wedding in Himachal Pradesh for 100–150 guests typically costs between ₹15 lakh and ₹40 lakh, with most of that going on the venue, rooms and catering. An intimate wedding for 30–50 guests can be done for well under ₹10 lakh, and a large, multi-day luxury wedding at a premium resort in Shimla or Manali can go well past ₹50 lakh. Here's how the budget breaks down, and where couples usually overspend or underspend.

## The biggest costs, in order

### 1. Venue and rooms (usually 35–50% of the budget)

In the hills, the venue and the rooms are usually one booking. Most resorts in Shimla, Manali, Kasauli and Dharamshala want you to book a block of rooms for your guests, often the whole property, for two or three nights. That's why the guest count matters so much more in Himachal than in a city wedding: you're not just feeding guests, you're housing them.

- Ask whether the venue charges a **room block** or a **full buyout**
- Check the **minimum guarantee** on rooms and plates
- Confirm **off-season rates**: the same resort can cost very different amounts in May and in August

### 2. Catering (usually 20–30%)

Hotel and resort listings in Himachal commonly quote per-plate rates starting around ₹1,500 and going past ₹3,000 for premium menus. Multiply by guests and by the number of meals across two or three days, because at a destination wedding you're providing breakfast, lunch and dinner, not just one reception dinner. A traditional Himachali dham for one function is a lovely touch, and local cooks (botis) can often prepare it.

### 3. Decor (usually 10–15%)

Mountain venues come with a backdrop that city venues spend lakhs trying to recreate, so many couples here keep decor lighter: floral mandap, fairy lights, a few focal installations. Remember that outdoor decor in the hills needs a weather backup (a tent or an indoor hall), and that has its own cost.

### 4. Photography and films (usually 5–10%)

For a destination wedding you want coverage of every function across two or three days, which usually means two photographers and a cinematographer. Ask whether the quote includes the team's travel and stay. A Himachal-based team usually costs less in travel than one flying in. See our [wedding photography cost guide](/blog/wedding-photography-cost-himachal-pradesh-guide/) for exactly what to ask for in a quote.

### 5. Guest travel and transport (often forgotten)

Most guests will arrive via Chandigarh, by road or flight. Shimla is about 3–4 hours from Chandigarh by road, Kasauli about 1.5 hours, Dharamshala about 5–6, and Manali about 8–9. Budget for:

- Tempo travellers or buses from Chandigarh or Delhi
- Local cabs between the hotel and venue if they're separate
- A buffer for weather delays in winter and monsoon

### 6. Everything else (10–15%)

Outfits, makeup, a planner, entertainment, the pandit, gifts and welcome hampers, and a contingency fund. Keep at least 10% unallocated. Something always changes in the mountains.

## Sample budgets

These are illustrative ranges to help you plan, not quotes:

- **Intimate wedding, 30–50 guests, one or two days:** roughly ₹5–12 lakh
- **Mid-size wedding, 100–150 guests, two or three days:** roughly ₹15–40 lakh
- **Large or luxury wedding, 200+ guests, premium resort:** ₹50 lakh and upwards

## How to bring the cost down

- **Pick the shoulder season.** Late September to November, and March to April, give good weather at lower rates than the May–June summer rush or the December holidays.
- **Choose a weekday wedding.** Resorts often have better rates midweek.
- **Shorten the guest list, not the experience.** In the hills every extra guest is an extra room for two nights.
- **Hire local where it makes sense.** Local caterers, decorators and photographers don't add travel to the bill, and they know the roads and the weather.
- **Combine functions.** Mehendi and sangeet on the same evening saves a full day of venue and catering.

## Where to have it

- **Shimla:** easiest access for big guest lists and lots of established venues. Colonial charm, deodar forests.
- **Kasauli and Chail:** closest to Chandigarh, quiet and green, good for mid-size weddings.
- **Manali:** dramatic snow-peak backdrops, best for smaller weddings where guests can manage the long road.
- **Dharamshala and McLeodganj:** Dhauladhar views, tea gardens and a calmer pace.
